Earlier today, the credits rolled on [The Fish Tale (2022)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt18952724/) and I found myself in awe of what unadulterated passion, perseverance, and proving the haters wrong could achieve. 

In what could be considered as a fictional biopic about a fish-obsessed individual, *Sakana-kun*, The Fish Tale paints a rosy portrait of a non-binary individual whose sole interest in life is ichthyology. They love fish. They want to surround themselves with fish. They catch and eat fish. In this singular pursuit, undaunted by naysayers and on the receiving end of the same old spiel about how the real world works, how they need to grow up and get a real job, Meebo is stubbornly childlike and innocent. 

But their passion is undoubtedly infectious, and it slowly rubs off on those around them. They change and inspire others without trying to, just by their innate nature. How can anyone do them any harm? All they want is just to admire our gill-adorned relatives from eons past, before we grew legs and decided to do taxes. The visuals are ethereal and dreamlike.

Shoutout to Meebo's mum for always standing by her daughter's quirkiness, as someone who didn't even really enjoy eating fish that much.
