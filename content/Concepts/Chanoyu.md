There is much to learn from the practice of the Japanese tea ceremony, or *chanoyu*, in observing and moving with intention in our world. 

Elevated to a form of art by legendary tea master [Sen no Rikyū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sen_no_Riky%C5%AB) in the 1500s, *chanoyu* is more than just an aesthetic ceremony where one consumes tea. It is an intentional practice, a form of meditation, a special dimension where hosts and guests are fully present and engage in a ritualistic exchange.

Every part of the *chaji* ceremony is intentional, ranging from the choice of earthenware and tea, to the curation of flowers and art in the *tokonoma*. Guests stroll through a manicured garden, wash their hands with a ladle, and enter the tearoom on all fours through a low, square door as a symbolic gesture that separates the inside from the outside world. 

There is a popular saying that goes with it too, [[Ichigo ichie (一期一会)]], which roughly translates into "for this once only", and it implies how every moment only ever occurs once—it cannot be recreated again, even with the same company and the same surroundings. 

To partake in the *chanoyu* is to be hyperpresent, to intentionally observe and appreciate all that is around us. To be grateful for the opportunity to live in the moment, to bask in the hospitality of your host, to enjoy this fleeting moment before it is gone forever.
